# Career Targeting Strategy (v3)

**Ryan Winzenburg** | September 2026

## The short version

The credible move is a **Director-level product-experience role with real product and operating-model authority** at a complex B2B software company. Senior Director and Head of Product Experience roles are the target when the posted scope matches what you actually own today. VP roles are a selective lane at growth-stage and mid-market companies where the mandate is executive. Remote-first from Denver, $225K+ base, full-time.

This is a search for a mandate, not a title. The mandate:

> **Lead product experience for a complex B2B business: the customer-facing product, the design organization behind it, and the operating model that gets work shipped. Use AI to make both the product and the organization faster and easier to run.**

Your differentiation in one line: **you make complex B2B products more usable while improving the system that produces them.** AI-enabled operations is the current mechanism. Product experience is the durable leadership category.

## Level architecture

Making Director the core of the funnel is not lowering the ambition. It is the bridge from a Senior UX Lead title to the scope you want, and it widens the funnel without changing the kind of work.

| Search time | Role family | Qualifies when | How to position it |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **60%** | **Director of Product Design, Director of Product Experience, Director of UX, Director of Experience Strategy, Director of Product Operations** | Complex enterprise B2B product, real cross-functional remit, and influence on roadmap or operating model | The core target. A Director at a 5,000-person company often carries more scope than a Senior Director at a 400-person one. Describe it as calibrated to company scale, never as a fallback. |
| **30%** | **Senior Director, Head of Product Experience, Head of Product Design, Head of Experience Strategy** | Portfolio scope, executive decision exposure, multi-team or team-building leadership, and responsibility for product or operating-model change | Apply when your actual Comcast Business scope maps to the job's decision rights, regardless of the title label. |
| **10%** | **VP Product Experience, VP Design, combined product-and-experience executive roles** | Growth-stage or mid-market B2B with an executive mandate to unify product experience, product practice, and AI-enabled execution | Warm introductions or unusually strong mandate fit only. Do not make these the funnel base. If the brief requires sole ownership of product revenue and pricing, you will be seen as a partner to the CPO, not the hire. |

Take a Director role if it expands scope, ownership, comp, executive exposure, or the path to a broader mandate. Reject it only if it is narrower than what you do now or limits you to downstream design production.

## The title bridge: translate scope, do not inflate title

Recruiters will see "Senior UX Lead" and anchor on it. The resume, LinkedIn, recruiter intro, and first conversation all have to answer the same question immediately: **what did this leader actually own?**

**Keep the formal title. Add a scope line under it.**

> **Senior UX Lead, Comcast Business**
> *Enterprise product experience and AI-enabled operating-model leadership across [product domain / portfolio]; lead a team of [N] designers and the UX capability program.*

**Make the first two bullets under the current role answer these five questions with verified facts:**

| Director-level signal | What to make explicit | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Organizational scale** | Direct and indirect team size, disciplines led, contractor or vendor footprint, hiring and mentoring | Separates a senior IC title from functional leadership |
| **Business scope** | Product portfolio, customer segments, user volume, revenue context, or named strategic initiative | Gives recruiters a scale to compare against the target role |
| **Decision rights** | Which roadmap, experience-principle, prioritization, governance, or operating-process decisions you owned or materially shaped | Directors are hired for decision quality, not just design quality |
| **Executive audience** | Leadership forums, executive reviews, planning cycles, senior stakeholder relationships | Establishes altitude above lead level |
| **Outcome evidence** | Adoption, task success, cycle time, rework, cost-to-serve, quality | Turns leadership claims into proof |

Use "led the experience strategy for," "established the operating cadence for," "partnered with product and engineering leadership to," and "shaped [named decision] by." Do not use "supported" or "oversaw" when you owned the decision. Do not claim people management, budget, or P&L unless it was real.

**Let the older roles carry the seniority.** The CVS / Aetna and Pitney Bowes outcomes are Director-scale regardless of the title at the time. They belong in the summary, not buried in the chronology.

**Use the ventures as range.** Casimir and Winzinvest show you have operated at the strategy level, just not inside a corporate title. One line in the About section: "In parallel, I have founded and operated..."

**Get the proof onto LinkedIn before the search scales.** Two published pieces and two recommendations that speak to scope (a VP-level Comcast Business stakeholder, a former peer at CVS or Pitney Bowes) do more for the title gap than any headline.

## Evidence spine

Three stories carry the search. Every resume version opens with them. One should be built out as a full case study in the format **before, intervention, measured outcome, operating-model implication**, for the portfolio and for interviews.

| Story | Claim as it stands | Verify before external use |
|---|---|---|
| **CVS / Aetna: task completion** | Task completion from 50% to 90% for 39M+ users during the $69B Aetna merger | Which workflow or product; baseline and post-launch measurement method; date range; your decision authority versus the team's; any secondary measure (support volume, cost-to-serve) |
| **Pitney Bowes: design system** | Design system across 300+ products, 50% reduction in development time | Adoption rate or coverage; how cycle time was measured and over what period; your ownership scope; team size |
| **AI-augmented delivery: cycle compression** | Product cycles from 18 months to 4 to 6 weeks | Which cycle, measured how; how much is attributable to AI versus other process changes; the quality-control method; repeatability. Pair with a concrete build (Kinlet, a working SaaS product shipped solo in about 30 days). |

Supporting evidence, used by version:

| Evidence | What it proves | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **Comcast Business:** team of [N] designers; UX maturity program moving the function from Level 2 to Level 3; 20-skill training library; AI-assisted designer workflows adopted inside a skeptical large enterprise | Current leadership scope, capability building, AI adoption under real constraints | The title bridge, every version |
| **Casimir Systems:** DoD SBIR work on AI decision support | Mission-aware AI product work in a regulated, high-consequence setting | Defense, public sector, regulated tech |
| **Winzinvest:** rules-based automated trading platform for RIAs and family offices | Automation with controls, trust, and economic consequences | Fintech, capital-markets software |
| **Camp Luxe:** short-term rental business operated since 2008 | Operator credibility. One sentence, in interviews, when asked | Interviews only |
| 25+ years across telecom (Level 3 / CenturyLink, Comcast Business), healthcare, federal (Department of Interior), and consumer platforms (MapQuest / AOL, Life Time / Athlinks) | The range that makes the systems approach credible | Summary line |

**Integrity rule.** Do not round or invent. If a number cannot be substantiated, use a scope measure instead: product coverage, user population, team count, stakeholder group, or a documented qualitative outcome.

### "Why now"

Asked in every first call. The answer is forward-looking, about the mandate, and does not explain the ventures. Draft, to be put in your own words:

> I've spent [X] years at Comcast Business building the UX function up a level of maturity and proving out AI-augmented delivery inside a large enterprise. The next thing I want to run is the product experience and the operating model for a whole B2B product organization. That mandate doesn't exist in my current seat, so I'm looking for the company where it does.

### Ventures

Have a two-sentence answer ready: they give you current operator practice in AI automation and complex decision workflows, and they do not compete with a full-time leadership role. Answer it when asked. Do not raise it first.

## What you are actually looking for

The right company has a problem, not just an open seat. It looks like one of these:

1. Acquired, fast-grown, or platform-heavy products that need a coherent customer experience and one way of working.
2. AI ambitions that need to become customer value and real internal productivity, not demos.
3. Product, design, engineering, sales, and customer success disagreeing on priorities, decision rights, or the path from discovery to delivery.
4. A legacy, technical, or regulated B2B business modernizing without losing reliability, domain depth, or trust.
5. A product org that wants design in the room before requirements are set and does not have that today.

When a posting describes one of these, apply even if the title is narrow.

**Deprioritize:** IC or pure UX/UI roles, design-system-only leadership, generic "AI product manager" roles, program management, innovation labs without operating authority, marketing-led CX programs detached from product decisions, and CPO roles at scaled product companies. Each has an exception (a principal role with real enterprise authority, a design system anchoring a multi-product mandate, an AI PM role that owns adoption and process redesign), but none is the default.

## Best-fit company profiles

| Profile | Why it fits |

## Screening: two stages

A posting cannot prove decision rights, operating-model authority, or AI sponsorship. Screen fast from the posting, then validate in the first call.

### Stage 1: posting screen (10 points, apply at 7+)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B2B complexity** | Consumer, agency, simple app | Mixed or unclear | Enterprise, platform, workflow, infrastructure, regulated, multi-stakeholder |
| **Scope** | Tactical execution | Team or product-area ownership | Portfolio, cross-functional, transformation, or org-level language |
| **Strategic proximity** | Requirements handed down | Partnership mentioned | Product strategy, roadmap, business outcomes, or executive partnership explicit |
| **AI / automation relevance** | Absent | Vague innovation language | AI, automation, intelligent workflows, or operating effectiveness is a real part of the brief |
| **Practical fit** | Fails level, location, comp, or work mode | One uncertainty | Meets all known constraints |

Any hard-constraint failure is a stop regardless of score.

### Stage 2: first-conversation validation (10 points, invest at 7+)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Decision rights** | Downstream execution | Informal influence | Clear responsibility or durable co-ownership of priorities and experience direction |
| **Operating-model authority** | None | Can recommend | Explicit remit to improve discovery, planning, measurement, or delivery |
| **Executive sponsorship** | No senior sponsor, vague need | Sponsor implied | Named executive sponsor and a visible business problem |
| **AI reality** | Marketing language | Exploratory activity | Funded initiative, defined workflow, success measures, accountable owner |
| **Leadership trajectory** | Narrow or static | Potential growth | Team, portfolio, or scope expansion tied to the business plan |

A strong company brand does not compensate for a 0 or 1 on decision rights.

**Questions that fill in Stage 2:**

1. What has to be visibly different twelve months after this person starts?
2. Where do product, design, engineering, and customer success lose time or make conflicting decisions today?
3. Does this leader shape product priorities before requirements are committed?
4. Which AI opportunity has an executive sponsor, and is it meant to change customer workflows, internal workflows, or both?
5. Which decisions does this role own, which does it influence, and which sit outside it?
6. How will success be measured?

If the answers describe a team that makes screens look good after decisions are made, stop. If they describe making a portfolio, a customer journey, and a product org work better together, invest.

## Positioning assets

**LinkedIn headline.** Recruiter search keys on current title, company, and role keywords. The headline needs all three, and it should match the 60% target, not the 10% lane:

> Senior UX Lead, Comcast Business | Director-level product experience leadership | Enterprise B2B, product operating models, AI-enabled delivery

Test a variant after two weeks and keep whichever produces more recruiter inbound. Avoid "transformation" in the headline; hiring managers discount it. Use it in the About section where numbers back it up.

**Recruiter and hiring-manager opening.**

> I lead product experience in complex B2B environments: the customer-facing product, the design org, and the operating model behind it. At CVS I took task completion from 50% to 90% for 39 million users during the Aetna merger. At Pitney Bowes a design system I led cut development time in half across 300 products. Now I use AI to compress delivery from months to weeks. I'm looking for a Director or Head role where that combined mandate is real, not a design seat that executes after decisions are made.

## Search alerts

Job boards handle Boolean differently. Keep queries flat: one title query paired with one keyword, several alerts rather than one long string.

| Platform | Setup |
|---|---|
| **LinkedIn Jobs** | Separate title alerts: `Director Product Design`, `Director Product Experience`, `Director UX`, `Director Experience Strategy`, `Director Product Operations`, `Head of Product Experience`, `Senior Director Product Design`. Add one keyword per alert: `enterprise`, `B2B`, `platform`, `AI`, `automation`, `workflow`. Filter to remote and Denver. |
| **Google Jobs / general search** | `Director Product Experience enterprise B2B`; `Head of Product Design AI workflow`; `Senior Director Experience Strategy platform`; `Director Product Operations AI enterprise` |
| **Company career sites and ATS** | Search titles first, then content terms independently: `product strategy`, `operating model`, `automation`, `AI`, `platform`, `enterprise`, `customer journey` |

Read the description, not the title. A posting that uses at least three of *enterprise, platform, operating model, workflow, AI, automation, product strategy, cross-functional, customer journey, design maturity, portfolio, modernization* is worth scoring.

## Weekly cadence

This runs alongside a full-time leadership job. Hold the quality bar and keep the volume sustainable.

| Activity | Weekly target | Standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Roles screened | 8 to 12 | Stage 1 score, two minutes each |
| Targeted applications | 3 to 4 | Stage 1 at 7+; tailored top third of the resume and a four-sentence note |
| Warm-network conversations | 2 to 3 | Ask about live organizational problems, leadership changes, and unposted mandates, not for referrals to posted jobs |
| Direct hiring-leader outreach | 1 to 2 | Open with a specific observation about their product complexity or product-org need |
| Recruiter conversations | 1 to 2 | Teach them the level architecture and the mandate. Decline generic design-leadership searches. |
| Public proof of work | 1 piece per month | Enterprise product experience, AI workflows, or product operating models. winzenburg.com and LinkedIn. |

Track each role with Stage 1 and Stage 2 scores, true mandate, decision-maker, and resume version used. After ten qualified applications and five conversations, look at which level and title family is producing real responses and shift the 60/30/10 split if the data says to.

## Decision rules

1. **Director is the core target. Senior Director / Head when scope matches. VP selectively.** Screen on scope, never on the word "Senior."
2. **Prioritize B2B complexity.** More stakeholders, more systems, more trust required means more differentiation.
3. **Insist on authority over both the experience and the conditions of execution.** A title without product or operating-model authority will not work.
4. **AI is the multiplier, not the title.** The best roles may not say "AI." They give you sponsorship to use it.
5. **One narrative.** Product experience leadership, with AI-enabled delivery and operating-model design as what makes it distinctive.
6. **Remote-first, Denver-based, $225K+ base, full-time.** Fixed.
7. **The ventures are range, not risk.** Answer the commitment question when asked and not before.
8. **Verify every number before it leaves the building.**
